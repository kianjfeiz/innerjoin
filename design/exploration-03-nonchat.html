dunes — exploration 03 · what it looks like when chat isn't the front door

The problem with a chat box on 200 files: you have to already know what to ask. A blank prompt puts the burden of knowing what's in the library on the person who dumped it there precisely because they didn't want to keep track. These three surfaces invert that — the app opens by telling you what it found, what's coming, and what looks off. Chat doesn't disappear; it moves to ⌘K, where you go when you have a specific question.

05

Why this shape

the case for briefing-first

It works when you're not there

A chat app is dead until you type. A briefing accrues value in the background — drop files Sunday, learn Monday that a deadline exists. It's the only surface that justifies a background engine.

the case for dossiers

The join becomes visible

Facts pulled from six files sit on one page, each traceable to its source. This is the thing chat can only describe and Obsidian can only hold — and no chat app gives you it.

the honest risk

It's more to build

A briefing needs date extraction, entity resolution, and change detection before it shows anything. Chat needs retrieval and a text box. This shape is v0.2 work packed into v0.1.

Same design language — sky wash, cyan→blue accent, no pink, gradient surfaces, pills, SF Pro + SF Mono metadata, dense rows. New patterns — briefing blocks with source lines, fact cards with provenance, entity dossier + timeline strip, sortable category table with CSV out, ⌘K palette that answers inline instead of opening a thread.
